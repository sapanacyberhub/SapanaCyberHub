₹1000 Crore HPZ Token Scam: How It Unfolded

What is the HPZ Token Scam?

The HPZ Token scam is one of the largest cyber-financial frauds uncovered in India in recent years. During the COVID-19 pandemic, when millions were confined to their homes and searching for online income opportunities, a fraudulent mobile application named HPZ Tokens surfaced across digital platforms.

Marketed as a cryptocurrency mining investment app, HPZ Tokens promised unusually high and “assured” returns by claiming that users’ money would be invested in bitcoin mining machines. In reality, no such mining infrastructure existed.

According to the Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI), the scam ultimately siphoned off nearly ₹1000 crore from unsuspecting investors across India, making it one of the most significant cyber scams linked to the pandemic period.

How the ₹1000 Crore Scam Worked

Investigators revealed that HPZ Tokens was operated through a complex and well-planned network designed to appear legitimate at every stage. The application was launched by Shigoo Technology Pvt Ltd, a company effectively controlled by Chinese nationals Wan Jun and Li Anming.

The app claimed that user investments were being used to purchase and operate bitcoin mining machines, with daily profits credited automatically. To build trust, early investors were shown fake dashboards displaying consistent earnings.

To attract a wider audience, victims were funneled in through multiple channels — including fake loan applications, online job offers, and misleading investment advertisements. Small initial withdrawals were sometimes allowed, creating a false sense of legitimacy.

Once deposits increased, withdrawals were delayed or blocked entirely. Funds were then laundered using India’s payment aggregation system and routed through more than 150 shell bank accounts before being moved overseas, making recovery extremely difficult.

Who Was Involved

The CBI chargesheet names 30 accused individuals, including two Chinese nationals believed to be the primary operators of the scam. Several Indian associates allegedly helped in setting up shell companies, opening mule bank accounts, and managing payment gateways.

Investigators stated that the operation relied heavily on cross-border coordination, encrypted communication channels, and layered financial transactions to evade detection by regulatory authorities.

Impact on Investors

The scam had a devastating impact on thousands of investors, many of whom had invested their life savings during the economic uncertainty of the pandemic. Victims ranged from salaried professionals and small business owners to students and unemployed individuals searching for online income.

In several cases, victims reported taking loans or borrowing from relatives after being convinced that the returns were guaranteed. When the app abruptly stopped functioning, users were left with no customer support, no access to funds, and little immediate legal recourse.

Common Scam Pattern Revealed by HPZ Token Case

Cybercrime investigators say the HPZ Token scam followed a pattern that is now commonly seen in large-scale online frauds. Understanding this pattern can help users avoid similar traps in the future.

  • Promises of fixed or guaranteed returns
  • Professional-looking mobile apps with fake dashboards
  • Small initial payouts to build trust
  • Heavy promotion through social media, job offers, or loan apps
  • Sudden withdrawal restrictions once large sums are invested

Experts warn that if an app or platform checks even two of the above signs, users should pause immediately and verify before investing.

What to Do If You Have Invested in Such a Scam

Authorities advise victims to act quickly if they suspect involvement in an online investment scam. While recovery is difficult, early reporting improves the chances of investigation and fund tracking.

  • Stop making any further payments immediately
  • Preserve transaction records, screenshots, and app details
  • Report the incident to the cybercrime portal (cybercrime.gov.in)
  • Inform your bank and request transaction monitoring
  • Avoid “recovery agents” claiming to get money back for a fee

Frequently Asked Questions

Can money lost in the HPZ Token scam be recovered?

Recovery depends on how quickly the case is reported and whether funds can be traced. In many large scams, full recovery is difficult, but reporting helps authorities prevent further damage.

Is HPZ Token still active?

No. The HPZ Token app and associated platforms were shut down following investigations. However, similar-looking apps may appear under different names.

Are all crypto apps scams?

No. However, legitimate crypto platforms do not guarantee profits. Any app promising fixed daily returns should be treated with suspicion.

How can I check if an investment app is genuine?

Verify company registration, look for regulatory approvals, read independent reviews, and avoid apps promoted aggressively through unsolicited messages.

Key Takeaway

The HPZ Token scam serves as a stark reminder that guaranteed returns do not exist in legitimate investments. Fraudsters thrive on urgency, trust, and lack of awareness.

Staying informed, verifying platforms, and questioning promises of easy money remain the strongest defenses against online financial fraud.

Key Lessons & Safety Tips

Cybercrime experts stress that the HPZ Token case highlights a critical lesson: any investment promising fixed or guaranteed returns — especially in cryptocurrency — should be treated as a red flag.

Users are advised to verify company registrations, avoid apps that lack regulatory approval, and remain cautious of schemes promoted aggressively during times of crisis. Authorities have repeatedly clarified that legitimate crypto investments do not guarantee profits and always involve market risk.
